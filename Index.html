Two sessions, one whiteboard each. The first traced hiring from the moment a manager says “I need someone” to the day the offer is accepted. The second traced everything after that — the week before joining, the first ninety days, the years in between, and the way people leave.

Put together they are one journey with 62 problems in it. Each one has the system that fixes it and, where it helps, the automation on top. Most of it costs nothing. What it costs is writing things down and then following them.

HR operations

Pre-boarding → alumni
36problems across 7 stages

Induction, the first ninety days, engagement, growth and exit. Everything that decides whether they stay.

The promise we make to every candidate

  • 48 hEvery action you take gets a response from us — apply, interview, offer.
  • 1 personA named recruiter with a phone number, from shortlist to your first day.
  • 3 roundsMaximum. 45–60 minutes each. You know the panel before you walk in.
  • A reasonEvery rejection comes with one. Every selection comes with a timeline.
  • No surprisesSalary band, documents and next steps are written down before the offer.

The promise we make to every employee

  • Day 1Laptop, seat, access and a written plan for your first ninety days, ready before you walk in.
  • Two namesA buddy and an HR contact you can call, from before you join until you're confirmed.
  • 30 minEvery month with your manager, on your agenda. Not once a year, attached to money.
  • Every monthRecognition, an engagement plan and a town hall where you can ask anything.
  • A good exitA real handover, a farewell, and a group you stay part of afterwards.

The pipeline

      Rollout: more with less

      Ship the free, discipline-only fixes first — they solve most of the candidate experience. Add paid tooling only where the free version has been running for a month and the gap is measured.

      Phase 1 — Systems only

      Weeks 1–2 · ₹0 · needs discipline, not budget

      Phase 2 — Automate the system

      Weeks 3–6 · low cost · WhatsApp API + workflow tool + LLM API

      Phase 3 — Scale what's measured

      Month 2–3 · paid · only where volume justifies it

      Where we keep it human

      • Lateral and senior candidates get a person on the phone, not a bot. AI prepares the recruiter; it doesn't replace the call.
      • The in-office final round: welcome, coffee, ten minutes of rapport before any question. No automation improves this.
      • Selling the company in the final round is the leader's job. AI only hands them talking points.
      • Rejection reasons are drafted by AI, approved and sent under a recruiter's name.

      Where AI is the only way

      • Bulk hiring: hundreds of applications per role can't each get a human touch three times. Automated comms and AI screening are what make the promise possible at volume.
      • Consistency: the same timeline, the same template, the same rubric — every time, for every recruiter.
      • Visibility: SLA breaches, stuck candidates and overdue feedback surfaced without anyone chasing.

      Rollout: more with less

      Phase 1 is writing, not buying — it fixes most of what's on this page and costs nothing but discipline. Phase 2 puts the writing on a calendar so it survives a busy month. Only in Phase 3 does anything get automated, and only where it has already run manually for a month.

      Phase 1 — Write it down

      Weeks 1–3 · ₹0 · discipline, not budget

      Phase 2 — Put it on a calendar

      Weeks 4–8 · ₹0 to low · cadence beats intention

      Phase 3 — Automate and measure

      Month 3+ · low, paid only where volume justifies

      What we'll hold ourselves to

      • Time to productiveDays until a joiner completes month one of their 30/60/90 plan. Today it's unmeasurable, because there's no plan.
      • 90-day attritionAnyone leaving inside three months is an onboarding failure, not a hiring one.
      • One-on-one completionPercentage of managers who held every monthly conversation. Completion only — never the content.
      • Exit reasons, trendedThe same reason appearing three times is a system problem, not three people.

      Where we keep it human

      • The farewell speech. A manager naming what someone contributed cannot be generated, and the team can tell.
      • The retention conversation when someone says they want to leave. That is a person and a decision, not a workflow.
      • The leader on the floor with no agenda, and the fifteen minutes of induction they show up for.
      • The buddy. The whole point is that it's a colleague, not a system.
      • Recognition: AI can draft the citation, but the name and the reason come from a manager who watched the work.

      Where AI earns its place

      • Writing what nobody has time to write: SOPs from a recorded walkthrough, 30/60/90 drafts, the weekly newsletter, the org map.
      • Remembering: forms at Day-7, buddy nudges, overdue KT, probation at week 12, the gifting order 30 days out. Every one of these fails today because a human had to remember it.
      • Reading at scale: six months of feedback and a year of exit interviews turned into themes in an afternoon.
      • Never deciding about a person. AI prepares the conversation. It does not score, rank or replace it.
